01 The problem

Nothing in diesel
contains nitrogen.
The air does.

Squeeze air hard enough and it passes 1600 °C. Above roughly 1300 °C the nitrogen and oxygen already in that air stop ignoring each other and combine. A diesel engine manufactures its own pollutant out of nothing but air and heat.

38,000premature deaths worldwide in one year, from diesels emitting more NOx than their standard allowed

02 Why it matters now

The limit collapsed. Demand did the opposite.

India skipped BS-V and went straight from BS-IV to BS-VI in April 2020. No engine meets that number by burning cleaner alone — it meets it with an SCR catalyst, and a catalyst is inert without DEF.

Sources: BS-VI vs BS-IV heavy-duty NOx reduction, Ministry of Environment. Market values, BlueWeave Consulting, India Diesel Exhaust Fluid (AdBlue) Market 2024–2030.
03 The product

The recipe is easy.
The window is not.

DEF is urea and water. Anyone can mix that. What it has to be is 32.5% urea, every single batch — outside a band 1.4 points wide the catalyst stops converting, or starts failing. That band is the whole business.

04 The chemistry

DEF never enters the engine.

It is metered into the hot exhaust downstream. Heat splits the urea into ammonia; the ammonia meets NOx on the catalyst surface.

4 NO + O2 + 4 NH3 → 4 N2 + 6 H2O
Urea decomposes to ammonia
